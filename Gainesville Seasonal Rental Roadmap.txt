The Critical 6-Month Exemption Threshold under F.A.C. Rule 12A-1.061
Florida Administrative Code (F.A.C.) Rule 12A-1.061 implements the transient accommodation exemption established in Florida Statute § 212.03(1)14. An accommodation is exempt from both state sales taxes and county Tourist Development Taxes only if the occupant enters into a bona fide written lease for continuous residence for a period longer than six months12.
The administrative rule establishes a strict calendar formula:
* First-of-the-Month Rule: Under F.A.C. Rule 12A-1.061(17)(b), for a lease commencing on the first day of a month, the term must run through the first day of the seventh consecutive month to qualify as a lease for a period longer than six months17.
* The November 1 – April 30 Trap: A lease executed from November 1, 2026, to April 30, 2027, spans exactly six calendar months (181 days). Under state administrative standards, this duration does not exceed six months17. Consequently, a November 1 to April 30 lease is fully taxable at the 12.50% rate ($2,550 on a $20,400 gross contract)15.
* Structuring for Complete Statutory Exemption: To secure an exemption from the 12.50% tax, the written lease agreement must be executed with an end date of May 1, 2027 (or later), spanning six months and one day17.

	Homestead Exemption and Save Our Homes (SOH) Cap Protection
If 4922 NW 81st Ave is the owner's primary residence benefiting from the Florida Homestead Property Tax Exemption, leasing the property requires strict compliance with Florida Statute § 196.0613:
* The 30-Day, Two-Year Rule: Fla. Stat. § 196.061(1) provides that renting a homesteaded property for more than 30 days per calendar year for two consecutive years constitutes an abandonment of the homestead exemption27.
* The January 1 Assessment Standard: Under Florida Statute § 196.031, property tax exemption and permanent residency status are established as of January 1 of each tax year28. A property under an active lease on January 1, 2027, faces statutory presumption of abandonment for that tax year28.
* Save Our Homes (SOH) Risk: Forfeiting the homestead exemption removes the constitutional 3% annual assessment cap27. The Alachua County Property Appraiser will reassess the property to its full Just Market Value, resulting in permanent, compounding increases in annual property taxes27.
* Statutory Penalties: Fla. Stat. § 196.011(9)(a) provides that failing to notify the property appraiser of disqualification can trigger a tax lien for all unpaid back taxes plus a 50% penalty and 15% annual interest28.
Property owners who temporarily lease their home during the winter and return to occupy it as their primary residence must maintain clear evidence of permanent residency (such as Florida driver's licenses, voter registration, vehicle registrations, and federal tax returns matching the address)13. Consulting with the Alachua County Property Appraiser prior to January 1, 2027, is recommended to ensure temporary seasonal absence is properly documented.

	Essential Florida Statutory Lease Contract Clauses
To ensure legal enforceability and protect the landlord asset under Florida residential landlord-tenant jurisprudence (Florida Statutes Chapter 83, Part II), the lease agreement must incorporate specific statutory clauses:
1. Security Deposit Holding & Disclosure Mandate (Fla. Stat. § 83.49)
Under Florida Statute § 83.49(2), the lease agreement or a separate written notice delivered within 30 days of receiving the security deposit must disclose the holding institution and include the statutory notice in full33:
"YOUR LEASE REQUIRES PAYMENT OF CERTAIN DEPOSITS. THE LANDLORD MAY TRANSFER ADVANCE RENTS TO THE LANDLORD'S ACCOUNT AS THEY ARE DUE AND WITHOUT NOTICE. WHEN YOU MOVE OUT, YOU MUST GIVE THE LANDLORD YOUR NEW ADDRESS SO THAT THE LANDLORD CAN SEND YOU NOTICES REGARDING YOUR DEPOSIT. THE LANDLORD MUST MAIL YOU NOTICE, WITHIN 30 DAYS AFTER YOU MOVE OUT, OF THE LANDLORD'S INTENT TO IMPOSE A CLAIM AGAINST THE DEPOSIT. IF YOU DO NOT REPLY TO THE LANDLORD STATING YOUR OBJECTION TO THE CLAIM WITHIN 15 DAYS AFTER RECEIPT OF THE LANDLORD'S NOTICE, THE LANDLORD WILL COLLECT THE CLAIM AND MUST MAIL YOU THE REMAINING DEPOSIT, IF ANY.
IF THE LANDLORD FAILS TO TIMELY MAIL YOU NOTICE, THE LANDLORD MUST RETURN THE DEPOSIT BUT MAY LATER FILE A LAWSUIT AGAINST YOU FOR DAMAGES. IF YOU FAIL TO TIMELY OBJECT TO A CLAIM, THE LANDLORD MAY COLLECT FROM THE DEPOSIT, BUT YOU MAY LATER FILE A LAWSUIT CLAIMING A REFUND.
YOU SHOULD ATTEMPT TO INFORMALLY RESOLVE ANY DISPUTE BEFORE FILING A LAWSUIT. GENERALLY, THE PARTY IN WHOSE FAVOR A JUDGMENT IS RENDERED WILL BE AWARDED COSTS AND ATTORNEY FEES PAYABLE BY THE LOSING PARTY.
THIS DISCLOSURE IS BASIC. PLEASE REFER TO PART II OF CHAPTER 83, FLORIDA STATUTES, TO DETERMINE YOUR LEGAL RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS."38
* Depository Terms: Funds must be held in a dedicated, non-interest-bearing account at a Florida financial institution32. The lease must state the name and address of the depository bank33.
* Deduction and Claim Timelines: Landlords have 15 days to return the full deposit if no deductions are made, or 30 days to provide an itemized notice of intention to impose a claim via certified mail32. Missing the 30-day certified mail deadline forfeits the right to make deposit deductions32.
2. Liquidated Damages & Early Termination Fee Addendum (Fla. Stat. § 83.595)
Pursuant to Florida Statute § 83.595, a residential lease may include a liquidated damages or early termination clause, provided the tenant signs a separate addendum offering two mutually exclusive choices39:
  

* Option A (Liquidated Damages Agreement): The tenant agrees to pay an early termination fee not to exceed two months' rent, provided they give up to 60 days' written notice39. Selecting this option releases the tenant from future rent obligations if the home remains vacant39.
* Option B (Common Law Remedies): The tenant declines liquidated damages, allowing the landlord to seek all remedies under Florida Statute § 83.595 (including holding the tenant liable for monthly rent as it comes due while making reasonable efforts to find a replacement tenant)39.
3. GRU Utility Consumption Cap Clause

Tenant agrees to reimburse Landlord for any overage within ten (10) business days of receiving an itemized GRU billing statement."
